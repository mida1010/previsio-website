4. Probabilistic nature and model limitations

  • Outputs are probabilistic estimates: they represent possible scenarios with statistical uncertainty, not certainties.
  • Prediction intervals express estimated statistical uncertainty under model assumptions: the actual outcome may fall outside the interval, potentially by a wide margin.
  • Directional probabilities are model- and data-conditioned estimates; they are not "true" or guaranteed probabilities. When not sufficiently reliable they may be suppressed or flagged as non-validated: the absence of a probability is itself information.
  • Past performance, whether actual or simulated (including backtesting), does not guarantee future results.
  • Models rely on simplifying assumptions that may not hold under real market conditions, in particular during abnormal regimes, extraordinary events, liquidity crises, structural discontinuities or regime changes.
  • Outputs may be wrong, biased or unstable and must not be used as the sole basis for any investment decision.

5. Risk warnings

Use of quantitative estimates involves significant risks that the professional User acknowledges and accepts, including:

  • Market risk: prices of financial instruments may move significantly and suddenly, resulting in losses up to the total loss of invested capital.
  • Model risk: quantitative models may be misspecified, fail to capture relevant phenomena, or produce systematically biased results.
  • Data-quality risk: incorrect, incomplete, misaligned data, survivorship bias, look-ahead bias or adjustment errors produce unreliable outputs.
  • Historical-data limitations: the availability and depth of historical series is limited and may not be representative of future regimes.
  • Overfitting risk: models calibrated on historical data may fit noise and show in-sample performance that does not replicate out-of-sample.
  • Regime-change risk: structural shifts in markets, monetary policy, regulation, microstructure or correlations may invalidate outputs.
  • Liquidity and extraordinary-event risk: under stress conditions, gaps, trading halts, geopolitical events or "black swan" events, outputs may be highly unreliable.
  • Forecast-interval uncertainty: intervals are dispersion estimates, not guaranteed bounds.
  • Probability-estimation limitations: reported probabilities are not calibrated in an absolute frequentist sense and may diverge from observed frequencies.
  • AI-generated text limitations: narrative commentary may contain inaccuracies, oversimplifications or misleading formulations and must not be used as a basis for decisions.
